## Overview

Keeping conference organizations healthy over decades.

## The generational challenge

Getting younger people involved is harder now.

**Why:**
- Fewer ICT students
- Schools have fewer IT teachers
- Education system struggles to prepare IT professionals

**Regional differences:**
- Asia has more younger people in both staff and attendance
- Europe shows aging in volunteers and attendees

**How people enter:**
- Non-technical people often get involved through someone they know
- Example: FOSSASIA's organizer came in through community, not technical background

## Recruitment

- **FOSDEM approach** - Ask best volunteers to join staff. Word of mouth. May miss people who don't attend.
- **Intentional recruitment** - Better than just hoping people show up

## Building intergenerational teams

- Give younger people real work, not grunt work
- Pair new members with experienced ones
- Document everything so learning isn't dependent on one person
- Include new people in planning, not just execution

**Different models:**
- **COSCUP:** Multiple leadership generations. Requires trust in newer leaders.
- **PyConf Korea:** Deliberately change members every 5 years. Harder transition but forces documentation.
- **OpenFest:** Wait for someone to do badly, motivated people take over. Risky but works.
- **DevConf.IN:** Seniors and juniors on small teams. Track good performers for core team.

## Keeping young people engaged

- Give them projects they care about
- Help build portfolios and recognition
- Social stuff matters - awards, gatherings, not just meetings
- Include them in decision-making

## Visibility and recruitment

Young people are on YouTube, TikTok, trending platforms. Not blogs.

- **FOSSASIA** - On all trending platforms, creating and sharing content
- **DevConf.IN** - University and company partnerships
- **OpenChain** - Write about experiences, people see it
- **Challenge:** Make open source visible where young people are without compromising values

## Organizational transitions

**Important:** Even if someone critical leaves, you survive. Takes more work, quality drops temporarily, but you're fine.

**FOSSASIA model for moving locations:**
- Core team stays constant
- Find local team members in new country
- Core team teaches locals
- Some locals get recruited to core team
- Process repeats, grows international team

## The hard parts

- **Admin work** - Accounting, contracts, compliance. Everyone hates it. Critical but hard to delegate.
- **Generational preferences** - Older people want tight groups. Younger want different social dynamics. Both valid.

## What works

- **Documentation** - Saves you when people leave. New people onboard fast.
- **Intentional recruitment** - Don't just hope
- **Delegate and trust** - Even when risky
- **Regional adaptation** - One approach doesn't work everywhere
- **Be visible** - Go where young people are
- **Inclusion takes work** - Doesn't happen by accident
- **Expect change** - People and demographics change
