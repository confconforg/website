## Overview

How to review proposals and pick speakers.

## Timing

- **Close the CFP before you start reviewing** - Don't start accepting talks until the deadline is past.
- **Tell speakers early** - 3 months before the conference is ideal. People need time for visas.
- **Early CFP for visa seekers** - Some conferences have a fast-tracked CFP just for people who need visas.
- **Start way earlier than you think** - Really. Plan this stuff months ahead.

## Reviewing

- **Use a scoring system** - It helps. When reviewers disagree wildly (one person gives 1, another gives 5), that's worth talking through.
- **Reviewer diversity matters** - If your review panel isn't diverse, your speaker lineup won't be either.
- **Not just experts** - Regular attendees review proposals well too.
- **Talk about the outliers** - When scores are all over the place, discuss it. Usually people agree once they explain their thinking.

### Examples of Review Scale

- **PromCon** - ~60 proposals, 20 speakers
- **FOSS.in (India)** - 300+ proposals, 15 reviewers  
- **FOSSASIA** - 500+ submissions, 200 speakers

## Picking talks

- **Shorter is better** - 25 minute talks beat 45-50 minute talks. Lightning talks are great.
- **Label by level** - Say which talks are beginner, intermediate, advanced. Adjust how many of each you pick.
- **Watch for first-timers** - Mark them. They need help.
- **Invite speakers** - Totally fine to invite someone you think would be good, if you know their work and it fits.
- **Help people prepare** - Give feedback on proposals if they need it. Especially first-timers and people from underrepresented groups.

## Making it fair

- **Public proposals** - Some conferences post all proposals and let people comment. That way good talks don't get accidentally rejected.
- **Anonymous submissions** - If proposals are visible, make them anonymous so reviewers aren't biased.
