## Overview

Ways to let people propose and run sessions on the fly.

## Lightning talks

- **Sign up anytime** - People can sign up just before or during the event if something inspires them.
- **Submit early, pick randomly** - COSCUP collects submissions during the conference and draws speakers out of a hat.
- **Pre-fill some slots** - Have a few known speakers lined up, then open the rest to whoever wants in.
- **Auto-advance slides** - ConfigManagementCamp uses 20 slides that auto-advance. Keeps talks short and energetic.

## Birds of a feather sessions

- **Need a topic** - FOSDEM lets people book a room, but they have to propose what they'll talk about.
- **See and sign up** - COSCUP lets people sign up after day 1, once they know what's happening and what they want to discuss.

## Other formats that work

**Open forum:**
- Put post-it notes on a wall. People pitch topics for 1 minute each.
- You can have 60 people in one room doing 2-40 concurrent topics.
- People drift between topics as they please. Good discussions happen.

**Discussion circles:**
- 8 chairs, one stays empty. When someone new sits down, someone leaves.
- Stops people from just watching passively.
- Side conversations are fine.

**Speed dating:**
- 2-3 minutes per person. Everyone introduces themselves in rotation.
- Name tags with what people are interested in or what they know.
- Good icebreaker.

## Making it accessible

- **Use plain language** - Not everyone knows what "BoF" means.
- **Pair people up** - Put newcomers with someone experienced.
- **Tours** - Have people show the new folks around, explain what's happening.
- **Keep it interactive** - Discussions, not lectures. Too many people just watching kills it.

## Things to try

- **Everyone presents a slide** - Each person makes one slide about what they want to discuss or need help with.
- **Classified ads** - A wiki or whiteboard where people post what they need and what they can help with.
- **Structured mentoring** - 1-on-1 time between experienced folks and newcomers.
