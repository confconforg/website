## Overview

What tools people use for ticketing, CFP, streaming, and talking to each other.

## Platforms

Everyone uses different stuff depending on budget and what they need:

### Ticketing & CFP

- **Pretalx** - Self-hosted CFP system used by FOSDEM, COSCUP, BalCcon. Open source. Customizable via plugins.
- **Pretix** - Ticketing system, often paired with Pretalx for an integrated stack.
- **Eventbrite** - Proprietary, high fees. Provides email to attendees but not always full data.
- **Eventyay** - Open source platform combining CFP, ticketing, and video. Less mature than Pretalx but growing.
- **Sessionize** - Used by CNCF conferences. Has API and automation. Free for free conferences.
- **Lu.ma** - Newer alternative mentioned by some conferences, though closed-source.

### Video & Streaming

- **Open Broadcast Studio (OBS)** - Free, reliable, can stream to YouTube or other platforms.
- **Venueless** - For live streaming and virtual participation.
- **Jitsi** - Free, self-hostable video calls.

### Communication

- **Discord** - Popular for real-time team coordination.
- **Matrix** - Decentralized, used by FOSDEM (though has limitations).
- **Email** - Eventbrite + custom systems. EmailOctopus for newsletters.
- **HedgeDoc/Etherpads** - Good for collaborative notes, but breaks with >2-3 concurrent editors. Google Docs is more reliable.

### Additional Tools

- **Volunteer Management** - COSCUP's open-source system is a good example.
- **Merchandise** - Shopify for non-profits + Printful for on-demand printing.
- **Donations** - Open Collective, direct bank transfers, WordPress plugins.
- **Support/Ticketing** - Zammad or RT (Request Tracker).
- **Certificates** - Certo (based on Open Badge, W3C standard). Useful for students.

## Tips

- Best tools are made by people actually running conferences. They understand the problems.
- Self-hosted stuff gives you more control but you have to maintain it.
- A lot of conferences run their own thing or heavily hack on commercial tools to make them work.
