## Overview

How different conferences select and schedule talks.

## Selection models

**FOSDEM** (65 tracks, ~1000 talks):
- Staff picks ~40 main talks
- 65 devrooms each have own CFP and managers
- Devrooms can recommend talks for main stage

**COSCUP** (23 tracks, 30 rooms, 340 talks):
- Invite community review teams per track
- Staff makes final decision

**OpenFest**:
- Program committee of outside community members
- Vote after CFP closes to catch blind spots
- 45-minute talks, 2-4 hour workshops

**DevConf.IN**:
- 5-6 tracks
- Require 40% new speakers
- Anonymous first-pass review

**Chemnitzer Linux-Tage**:
- No fixed tracks
- Talks are 30, 60, or 90 minutes
- Custom system for everything

## Talk formats

- **20 minutes:** Intro talks and pitches
- **40 minutes:** Standard length
- **Lightning talks:** 5 or 15 minutes
- **Workshops:** 1 to 4 hours

**Lightning talk selection methods:**
- Chemnitzer Linux-Tage: Postcards, random selection
- OpenFest: First come, first serve sign-up
- FOSSASIA: Pre-record, publish automatically
- OpenChain: Attendee voting

Keynotes and invited talks are staff picks, sometimes intentional to fill gaps or balance representation.

## Common challenges

- **Abstract quality:** Not all good speakers write good abstracts
- **Recycled talks:** Some events limit repeats to 30%. Others ask for recordings.
- **Topic trends:** Everyone wants Kubernetes and DevOps. Harder to find other topics.
- **Celebrity speakers:** Some draw crowds regardless of topic quality
- **New vs returning:** DevConf.IN targets 40% new speakers. Hard to balance. Wanted 50%, but was impossible
- **Prolific submitters:** Some people submit dozens of proposals (DevConf had one with 14)
- **AI-generated submissions:** Starting to appear. Some events detect or flag.
- **Visa requirements:** Need advance notice and invitation letters

## Review process

- **Anonymous review** - At least for first pass
- **Community input** - Voting or public review catches blind spots
- **Check history** - Look at previous talks and publications
- **Scale reviewers** - DevConf.IN uses 1 reviewer per 10 talks

## Scheduling

- **Speaker availability:** FOSDEM asks, many don't bother
- **Slot selection:** DevConf does first-come first-served
- **Track conflicts:** Hard to avoid with parallel sessions

## What works

- Clear criteria applied consistently
- Being flexible about what's interesting (not just trendy)
- Help speakers with visas, abstract writing, community intro
- Pick diverse speakers, not just people you know
- Multiple reviewers instead of one person deciding
