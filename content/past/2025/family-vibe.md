## Overview

How to build community and make people feel welcome at a conference.

## Before the event starts

- **Speaker dinners** - Get people together before the conference. Speakers meet organizers, then they all know each other during the event.
- **Community days** - Have a smaller event the day before. Low pressure, people can get to know each other.
- **Group travel** - Carpooling works. Buses with all the speakers works. Just watch out for privacy issues.

## During the conference

- **Single track** - Everyone in the same talks means more mingling and less FOMO.
- **Extra time** - Add a half day with no sessions, just time for people to talk.
- **Breaks** - Lots of them. Make sure there are places to sit and chat. Lunch should be relaxed.
- **Designated connectors** - Pick some experienced people to help newcomers meet others.
- **Show up** - Let organizers and speakers be visible, joking around. Don't make it us vs. them.
- **Keep it real** - Let conversations happen naturally.

## On diversity

- **Diverse review panels** - If your speaker lineup isn't diverse, look at who's reviewing proposals and how you're promoting the call. Not the submissions.
- **Regular people as reviewers** - You don't need only experts. Attendees give good feedback too.
- **Track numbers** - Some conferences track who's submitting. Useful to see where you're weak.
- **Language** - Decide if you're multilingual or local. Both are valid, but plan accordingly.
- **Treat staff well** - Caterers, volunteers, cleaners. How they're treated sets the tone for everyone.
